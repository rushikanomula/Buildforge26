    How the prototype works

    A safety layer that sits above routing. Every 10 simulated seconds it re-reads the live state, forecasts whether the mission will miss its limit, explains why, and checks whether any clinically eligible option prevents the miss.

    1. Observe. Speed on every road segment and its trend over the last minute, known closures and rain from event feeds, and each hospital's ambulance queue with the age of that data. When a hospital feed stops, the queue is treated as unknown, not normal, and uncertainty widens.
    2. Predict. 200 possible futures are simulated for the remaining route. Each one projects congestion forward using the usual evening profile, rain, the current slowdown's trend, and spread from neighbouring roads, with random noise and the chance of a new incident. Time to care is drive time plus the expected ED handover wait. The share of futures that exceed the limit is the risk. In the full system, a trained GNN + LSTM replaces this forecaster.
    3. Explain. The expected trip is re-run with one factor removed at a time: the current slowdown, closures, rain, the ED queue, the route choice, and the usual rush-hour bottleneck. The minutes that disappear are that factor's contribution.
    4. Find a fix. Alternative routes to the same hospital and the best route to every hospital that can treat this patient type are scored on the same 200 futures, so the comparison is fair. Hospitals without the right unit are never suggested.
    5. Recommend, don't act. A change is suggested only if it saves at least 3 minutes and lowers the risk. The dispatcher approves or dismisses it. Dismissed suggestions stay quiet for 3 minutes unless risk climbs further.
    What is simulated: the road network is a schematic of real Hyderabad localities with approximate distances; traffic, incidents and hospital queues come from a simulator; hospitals A to F are fictional. What is real: the prediction, attribution, eligibility and recommendation logic, and the replay evaluation method.
